1. Introduction to CTPS HVDC

The Chandrapur Thermal Power Station (CTPS) is one of India's premier energy generation hubs. The HVDC (High Voltage Direct Current) section at Chandrapur is a critical terminal of the 1500 MW Chandrapur-Padghe link. This visit focused on the transition from AC generation to long-distance DC transmission.

The HVDC technology used here enables bulk power transmission over 736 km with significantly lower losses compared to conventional HVAC systems. As of 2026, the station utilizes advanced monitoring systems and upgraded cooling mechanisms to handle peak loads.Chandrapur Thermal Power Station (CTPS) is one of the largest and most important coal-based power generating stations in Maharashtra, operated by Maharashtra State Power Generation Company Limited (MAHAGENCO). It is strategically located near Chandrapur city due to the availability of coal resources and water supply, making it an ideal site for large-scale thermal power generation. As of 2026, the total installed capacity of CTPS is approximately 2920 MW, generated through multiple units of varying capacities operating under high efficiency conditions. The plant uses coal as the primary fuel, which is sourced from nearby coal mines, ensuring a continuous and economical fuel supply. CTPS is equipped with modern boilers, turbines, and generators designed to operate at high pressure and temperature, improving overall plant efficiency. The electricity generated is transmitted through a robust transmission network operating at 220 kV and 400 kV voltage levels, ensuring reliable power delivery across Maharashtra

2. Technical Process Workflow

AC Input

400kV AC incoming from CTPS generators into the converter transformer yard.

Rectification

Thyristor valves convert AC to ±500kV DC in a controlled environment.

Filtration

AC and DC filters remove harmonics and ensure smooth wave profiles.

Transmission

Bulk DC power sent to Padghe terminal via bipolar transmission lines.

3. Detailed Thyristor Valve Analysis

The "Valve Hall" is the heart of the HVDC system. We observed the massive Thyristor quadrivalve structures suspended from the ceiling to ensure seismic stability and electrical insulation.

  • Valve Composition: Each valve contains multiple series-connected thyristor levels.
  • Triggering: Fiber-optic cables are used for light-triggered thyristors (LTT) to ensure isolation from high voltage.
  • Cooling: De-ionized water cooling systems circulate through the heatsinks at the thyristor level.

5. On-Site Observations & Methodology

Control Room Procedures

The students witnessed the SCADA-based control of active and reactive power. The interface shows real-time temperature of thyristor valves and the status of DC circuit breakers.

Maintenance Protocol

Inspection of the surge arresters and the grounding electrodes. Discussion on the importance of the metallic return and earth return modes during maintenance of one pole.
